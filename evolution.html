The evolution of this site

This site started from zero — a literally blank page — and grew one iteration at a time, through ideas tried, changed, thrown away and picked back up again: more than 80 real pull requests, each one a small creative experiment rather than a plan fixed in advance.

  1. 🕰️

    Where it all began

    August 30, 2026 — the first commit, squeezed into spare moments around a day at work.

  2. 🌱

    v1: the origins

    Starting from zero: a bio, a few cards, tap-to-flip.

    • Bio, role chips, and tap-to-flip cards instead of hover
    • Rewritten Music/AI/Gaming copy, and the README's first link to the site
  3. 🕸️

    v2: the interactive graph is born

    A second version of the site takes shape around a draggable node graph, with themes and translation.

    • A second version of the site, with a draggable node graph replacing the classic sections
    • Detail panel as a fixed overlay, hamburger navbar, light/dark theme and IT/EN translation
    • Plenty of stability fixes along the way — flip cards, caching, overlapping nodes
  4. 🎨

    The graph takes over

    The interactive graph gets more polish: themes, voice, a second project.

    • Light theme and English as the default, and a second project (Cerebro Engine)
    • Sub-nodes arranged in a star, a more natural read-aloud voice, a new accent color
  5. 🫧

    dot is born

    A mascot starts hopping around in the hero — then takes over the whole page, merging the site's two versions into one.

    • A mascot starts hopping in the hero, then roams freely across the whole page
    • Gains real throw physics, its own sound, and grows a little on every tap
    • v1 and v2 merge: the interactive graph becomes the site's only version
  6. 💥

    dot comes alive

    Physics, impacts, clones that split and merge back together: dot becomes a little creature with a personality of its own.

    • Popping, page-element impacts, and a double-click that splits dot into colored clones
    • Clones merge back together on approach; dot is born and reborn from the logo's own dot
    • A handful of fixes along the way — color, drag feel, mobile behavior
  7. 📰

    dot reads the news

    dot starts reporting what's going on — first just GitHub, then a shared pool of public feeds.

    • dot starts showing GitHub project updates in its speech bubble, then a badge for when there's something new
    • Each dot had its own dedicated feed at first — later, all of them share one interleaved pool
    • The bubble grows and becomes clickable, opening the source of whatever it just read
  8. ✨

    Ongoing polish

    More realistic physics, fresher feeds, and a string of bugs found (and fixed) by watching dot closely.

    • Realistic hop and throw physics; the speech bubble always stays fully on screen
    • Fresher feeds — weather, NASA, Wikipedia trending — instead of old, stale news
    • A string of real bugs found and fixed by watching dot closely: a memory leak, a stuck news cycle, and more
  9. 😤

    The projects grow up

    Dot World gets its final name, Cerebro Engine gets a page of its own, and dot starts showing (literally) some nerves.

    • The RPG minigame becomes "Dot World", with a three-dot icon that has eyes; Cerebro Engine gets its own dedicated page with its flow diagram, instead of a lightbox
    • Poked for too long, dot properly loses it: it grows, turns red, and its final explosion topples the whole page and pops its clones along with it
    • Another memory leak found and fixed — clones' AudioContexts were never closed — and Cerebro Engine's diagram becomes readable on any screen
